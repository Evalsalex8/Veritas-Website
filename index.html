Understand the Issue

What Is Greenwashing?

Greenwashing happens when a business uses environmental messages, designs, or claims to make a product or company seem more eco-friendly than the available evidence supports.

Greenwashing is misleading people about how environmentally friendly a product, service, or company really is.
Look Beyond the Advertisement

Common Greenwashing Tactics

The problem isn't just using green logos or talking about saving the planet. The real issue is when an ad tricks you into thinking a product is way better for the earth than it actually is.

“100% Green”

An ad claims a product is perfectly safe for the earth, but doesn't actually explain how or why.

Ask: What does “green” actually mean?

Selective Facts

A company brags about one small good thing they do while hiding a bunch of bad things. For example, a fast-food place might brag that they switched to paper straws, but completely ignore the fact that the entire cup is still made of unrecyclable plastic.

Ask: What details are they leaving out?

Fake Eco-Vibes

NUsing earth-toned colors and nature words just to make the product look clean and safe.

Ask: Are they just using a cool design to trick me?
Be a Smarter Consumer

Think Before You Trust a Claim

You do not need to be an expert. Just ask a few simple questions to see if an ad is telling the real story or just faking it.

1

Is the claim specific

Check for broad words like "eco-friendly" or "natural" instead of real, specific details.

2

Is there evidence

Check whether the company provides reliable information supporting the claim.

3

Who verified it

Look for credible certifications and understand what they actually certify.

4

Check everything about the product

A company might use recycled plastic for the bottle but fill it with chemicals that harm the earth.
